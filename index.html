Performing Arts Participation in Australia

Exploring who goes to live performances, how attendance has changed
between 2017-18 to 2021-22 and where audiences are from.


This page visualizes Australians' participation in performing arts events
using data from Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) and NSW Government.
These include live music, theatre, musicals, dance and others.

Who participates in performing arts events in Australia?

| Popular performing arts

Live music is the most popular at about 18 in 100 Australian adults.
Whereas, dance performance is the least popular at about 5 in 100 Australian adults.

Source: ABS, Cultural and creative activities, 2021-22.

| Which age group is amongst the audience?

Live music has concentrated amount of 18 to 24 year olds.
While, theatre is the steadiest across all age groups.

Furthermore, across all performing arts, people aged 75 and over are the least likely to go.

Source: ABS, Cultural and creative activities, 2021-22.

| Do women go more than men?

For every type of performing arts, women do go more than men.
Women are found to almost be twice as likely to go to dance performances than men.
Whereas, live music is the most even between the two genders.

Source: ABS, Cultural and creative activities, 2021-22.

What happened to the attendance?

| Losing audiences over time

For every type of performing arts, the attendance nearly halved
between the two years for both adults and children.

Hover over a circle to see each art form's percentage change.

Sources: ABS, Attendance at Selected Cultural Venues and Events, 2017-18;
ABS, Cultural and creative activities, 2021-22.

Where do people go?

| Where the adults are most likely going

The Northern Territory has the highest percentage of 38.9%
Whereas, the lowest are NSW and Victoria at around 26%.

Source: ABS, Cultural and creative activities, 2021-22.

| Where the participants are

New South Wales and Victoria has the biggest circles which translates to having the most people that participated.

But, a big circle does not automatically mean they often participate in performing arts. Those states just have more people.

Source: ABS, Cultural and creative activities, 2021-22.

| A few states hold most of the audience

When viewed with the previous chart above, Victoria has the second largest audience with 1392 thousand but the lowest attendance rate of 26.5%. Whereas, Northern Territory has the smallest audience of 57 thousand but the highest attendance rate of 38.9%.

Note: These totals follow population, so they show where audiences are from and not where people go most often.

Source: ABS, Cultural and creative activities, 2021-22.

| The Before and After

Every state's attendance dropped. Across Australia,
the share dropped from 53.7% to 29.5%.

Source: ABS, Cultural and creative activities, 2021-22.

| Who ranked up, who ranked down?

Every state's ranking was shuffled but interestingly, Victoria fell down the ranking from top 3 to last place. Whereas, the Northern Territory rose up the ranks from second last to first place.

Note: ABS reports a margin of error of about 4.1 points for the Northern Territory. The 2.8 points lead over Western Australia is within that margin. So, treat the top rankings as close.

Source: Source: ABS, Cultural and creative activities, 2021-22.


| How much did each state fell?

Every state lost its ground. Victoria fell from 55.7% to 26.5%, while the Northern Territory fell from 49.0% to 38.9%. This visualizes every state as an equal sized tile, so smaller states such as the Australian Capital Territory and Tasmania are more visible. The darker the tile, the bigger the fall.

Source: Source: ABS, Cultural and creative activities, 2021-22.




Are participations coming back?

| Attendees rising back stronger in New South Wales

The attendance in New South Wales dropped from 7.2 million in 2019
to 2 millions in 2020. The attendance recovered back to 9.2 million
in 2023. This is likely the result of the pandemic.

Source: Data.NSW, Number of attendees to live performances, NSW
